> The Cooper-pair breaking efficiency quantifies the effectiveness with which incident energy, such as that delivered by photons or phonons, is converted into quasiparticles-a process central to the response of superconducting pair-breaking detectors. We measure the pair-breaking efficiency in two far-infrared kinetic inductance detectors (KIDs) operating at 4.3 THz and 15 THz by comparing photon noise and thermal generation-recombination noise. Both KIDs are lumped-element devices consisting of a tantalum interdigitated capacitor in parallel with an aluminum inductor meandered line, which also acts as a low-volume absorber. The measured pair-breaking efficiencies are 0.42 and 0.07 for the 4.3 THz and 15 THz devices, respectively.
